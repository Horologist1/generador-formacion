## Handling customer data safely
tipo: portada

> Welcome. Every day we handle names, addresses and phone numbers that belong to our customers.
> In the next few minutes you'll learn how to keep that information safe, and what to do when something goes wrong.

## Why it matters
tipo: cifra
cifra: 4 in 5
texto: data incidents at Brightline last year were caused by simple human mistakes.

> Last year, four out of five data incidents at Brightline were not caused by hackers.
> They were simple human mistakes: the wrong recipient, the wrong attachment, the wrong place.

## The three golden rules
tipo: contenido

- **Share only what is needed**: send the minimum data the task requires.
- **Use approved tools**: company email and shared drives, never personal accounts.
- **Check before you send**: recipient, attachment and access settings.

> There are three golden rules that prevent almost every incident.
> {1} First, share only what is needed. If a colleague needs one address, don't send the whole customer list.
> {2} Second, use approved tools. Customer data never goes to personal email or personal cloud storage.
> {3} And third, check before you send. Ten seconds of checking saves weeks of trouble.

## Spot the mistakes
tipo: correo

```correo
From: Sam Porter <sam.porter@brightline-logistics.com>
To: [[sam.porter.home@gmail.com|Customer data sent to a personal account]]
Subject: Customer list for the weekend

Hi Sam,

Sending this to myself so I can finish the delivery plan from home.

[[btn:customers_FULL_export.xlsx|The full export: 12,000 customers, when only 40 were needed]]

[[Link: anyone with the link can view|A public link: anyone who gets it can open the file]]
```

> Here is a real-looking email. It seems harmless: someone just wants to work from home.
> {1} But look at the recipient: it's a personal Gmail account, outside the company.
> {2} The attachment is the full customer export, twelve thousand people, when the task only needed forty.
> {3} And the sharing link is public, so anyone who receives it can open the file.
> Three mistakes in one short email, and none of them was intentional.

## If something goes wrong
tipo: contenido

- **Act fast**: recall the email or remove the shared link right away.
- **Report it** to privacy@brightline-logistics.com within one hour.
- **Don't hide it**: early reports turn incidents into simple fixes.

> Mistakes happen. What matters is what you do next.
> {1} First, act fast: recall the email, or remove the shared link.
> {2} Then report it to the privacy team within one hour. They will tell you what else is needed.
> {3} And don't hide it. Reporting early turns a serious incident into a simple fix.
