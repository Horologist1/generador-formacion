## Spotting phishing emails
tipo: portada

> Hi, and welcome. In the next few minutes you'll learn how to recognise a phishing email, and above all, what to do when you get one.
> You don't need to be an IT expert. You just need to know where to look.

## Why it matters

> Last year, seven out of ten security incidents at Grupo Alvento started in exactly the same way: with an email.
> Nobody hacked our servers. Someone, in a hurry, clicked where they shouldn't have. And that could happen to any of us.

## The three warning signs
tipo: contenido

- **Odd sender**: the name looks familiar, but the address doesn't add up.
- **Urgency or threats**: "today", "immediately", "your account will be locked".
- **Unexpected links and attachments**: you're asked to click, download or enter your password.

> Almost every phishing email has at least one of these three signs.
> {1} The first one is the sender. The name you see might say IT Support, but what matters is the address behind it.
> {2} The second is urgency. If an email rushes you or threatens you, it's precisely so that you don't stop and think.
> {3} And the third: links or attachments you weren't expecting, especially if they ask for your password.

## A real example
tipo: correo

```correo
From: Alvento IT Support <[[support@alvent0-it.com|The domain uses a zero instead of an "o"]]>
To: you
Subject: [[URGENT: your account will be locked today|Urgency and a threat in the subject line]]

Hi,

We have detected unusual activity on your account. If you don't verify it within the next 2 hours, your access will be permanently blocked.

[[btn:Verify my account|The button leads to a site that imitates ours and asks for your password]]

Kind regards,
The Support Team
```

> Take a look at this email. At first glance it seems normal. But let's look closer.
> {1} Look at the sender's address: Alvento is spelled with a zero instead of an o.
> {2} The subject line rushes you, and threatens to lock your account today.
> {3} And the button takes you to a page that imitates ours, to steal your password.
> Three warning signs in a single email.

## What to do if you get one
tipo: contenido

- **Don't click** or download anything.
- **Press "Report phishing"** in the Outlook toolbar.
- **Already clicked?** Call extension 4040 straight away. Don't worry: reporting it early fixes almost everything.

> If you're suspicious of an email, the rule is simple.
> {1} First, don't click on anything, and don't download any files.
> {2} Then press the Report phishing button in the Outlook toolbar. That way the security team can block it for everyone.
> {3} And if you've already clicked, there's no shame in admitting it. Call extension forty forty as soon as possible. The sooner you tell us, the easier it is to fix.

## Summary
tipo: cierre

- Check **the address**, not just the sender's name.
- Be wary of **urgency** and unexpected links.
- When in doubt: **Report phishing**. Already clicked? **4040**.

> And that's it. Remember: check the sender's address, be wary of anything that rushes you, and when in doubt, report it.
